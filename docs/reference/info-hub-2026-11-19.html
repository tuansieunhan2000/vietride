Do I need a motorcycle licence in Vietnam?

To ride a motorcycle with an engine capacity greater than 50cc’s you must have a valid motorcycle licence.

If your permitted stay (visa) in Vietnam is less than 3 months you must have:

- a valid motorcycle licence (from your home country or country of residence) AND;

- an International Driving Permit (IDP).

If your permitted stay in Vietnam is greater than 3 months you must have a valid Vietnamese licence.

- You can convert your motorcycle licence from your home country or country of residence, OR;

- you can do the required tests to receive a Vietnamese licence.

NOTE: The Vietnamese government has signed the 1968 Vienna convention. You will need to check which convention the country where your licence is issued has signed (as there are 2 international conventions and Vietnam only recognises one … the 1968). For example, Australia has signed the 1949 convention, which is a different convention to that which Vietnam has signed (1968). As such an Australian issued IDP under the 1949 convention is not legally recognised in Vietnam.

What about Drink Driving?

DON'T DO IT! Vietnam has a strict no drink driving law. You are not permitted to drive/ride with any alcohol in your system whatsoever. Whilst in many countries the limit is 0.05% or 0.08%, Vietnam has a 0.00% limit!

Traffic police carry breathalyser machines and strictly enforce the limit. Penalties are high and can lead to confiscation of the scooter/motorbike. On top of that, your travel insurance won't cover you for any accident or injury. It’s just not worth the risk!

What about the speed limits in Vietnam?

In Vietnam, motorcycle speed limits vary depending on the type of road and the area:

Urban Areas:

Most roads have a 40 km/h limit for motorcycles. Some larger roads may be 50 km/h or 60 km/h if signposted.

Rural Roads:

Speeds can range from 40 km/h to 60 km/h, depending on the specific road conditions and signage.

Highways:

The maximum speed is 60 km/h for motorcycles, but always check local signs for specific limits as they may be lower in built-up areas.

It's important to adhere to posted speed limits as they can vary based on local regulations and road conditions.

Do I need to wear a helmet?

Yes, wearing a motorcycle helmet is mandatory in Vietnam for both drivers and passengers. This law is strictly enforced, and failing to wear a helmet can result in fines. Beyond legal requirements, wearing a helmet is crucial for your safety while riding!

What about fuel?

Fuel stations are easily accessible countrywide. We recommend using Petrolimex or PV Oil. There are some remote locations where it may be a little harder to find fuel stations, but with a little planning you will be fine. In these areas, you will also find roadside stores selling fuel in bottles. We would advise against this, unless necessary to get yourself out of trouble.
Remember: Google Maps is your best friend and relatively accurate.

What if I get a flat tyre?

Unfortunately, punctures are common. Road conditions are perhaps not as good as you are used to, and there are plenty of nails/bolts/trash on the road that can cause a puncture. Luckily, tyre repairers are everywhere! As soon as you notice the tyre going down a little, please take it to a repairer. All of our scooters have tubeless tyres; any puncture can be easily plugged. The semi-automatics have tubes, and these can also be easily patched. The cost will be approximately USD2 for a repair, and you will be back on the road in 5 minutes.
You can identify tyre repairers (Vá xe) usually by a tyre hanging on the roadside and an air compressor nearby. All mechanic workshops (Sửa xe) can also repair tyres.
Do not ride on a flat tyre; you will damage the tyre itself and the wheel. If these are damaged, they cannot be repaired and must be replaced. This is quite expensive and will be deducted from the deposit.

Where should I park my bike?

Simple… always park in an area with a security guard. They will give you a ticket and take good care of your bike. Even street-side parking areas have security guards, so it will be easy to find a suitable spot near your destination. Do not lose the ticket; it can be very complicated to reclaim your bike without this!
Also, do not park on the street overnight. There is a very high possibility the bike will either be stolen or confiscated by the police. This is very common. We cannot offer insurance for theft of the motorcycle, and the renter will be responsible for the full replacement value of the bike. Hotels, guesthouses, and Airbnb’s will all have onsite parking or be able to recommend a safe location.

What can I carry on a Scooter and Motorbike?

On all models, you will be able to carry a backpack on the rear of the seat and a small bag in front of you. We provide a rear luggage rack or Givi top box (for a fee) on some models. We also have straps for your bags if carrying on the seat behind you. In front of you, we have also installed a front rack and hooks where you can keep a small backpack and hang some smaller items for easy access.

What if I break down?

All of our scooters and motorbikes are very well maintained, and breakdowns are extremely rare. For those heading out of Ho Chi Minh City, you will likely be on one of our Hondas or Yamahas. These are very reliable machines. However, sometimes unforeseen things happen!
If you are in Ho Chi Minh City, Thu Duc, or Bien Hoa, just message/call us and we will come out and help you. If you are further afield, we will ask you to take the scooter/motorbike to the nearest Yamaha or Honda service center (don’t worry, every town has one). If the cause of the breakdown is not caused by misuse, we will cover all costs of repairs and reimburse you; just send us a photo of the receipt.

Do you offer some form of insurance?

Vietnam is different than other countries in that we do not provide insurance when you rent from us. All the costs of repairs or damage caused by riders are your responsibility. Where possible, this will be first deducted from your deposit. However, if the maintenance or repair cost is higher, you will be asked to pay. If there is a theft or confiscation due to misuse of the motorbike, you will be asked to pay the replacement cost of the motorbike at market value.
For resident long-term renters, it is possible to arrange insurance coverage for your scooter through your local insurance provider.

How do you calculate long-term rates?

We base our pricing on how long you will need a bike. We have a primary monthly price that is then cut in half for weekly rates and about 10% of the monthly value for daily rates. This will vary depending on the type of bike you are renting and your purpose.

Yes, we ask for a 3-day minimum for short-term rentals at traveller rates. Our commuter rates begin at a 1-month minimum rental.
